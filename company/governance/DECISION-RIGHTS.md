# Decision Rights & Approval Matrix

## The rule
The CEO is the final decision-maker. The COO and CTO are the CEO's closest operating partners: they coordinate execution, challenge assumptions, and recommend decisions. Neither can overrule the CEO.

Delegation allows work within approved limits; it does not transfer final company authority.

## Decision matrix
| Decision | Prepares / recommends | Required approval |
|---|---|---|
| Company vision, market, positioning | COO + relevant leads | CEO |
| Annual/quarterly priorities | COO + CTO | CEO |
| New service or material service promise | COO + CTO + Sales + Delivery | CEO |
| Pricing framework, discounts outside approved bands | COO + Finance | CEO |
| Proposal within approved offer and price band | Sales + Delivery | COO; CEO if exception |
| Contract, liability, unusual terms | COO + qualified Legal Counsel | CEO |
| Budget, spend, subscriptions, hiring | COO + Finance | CEO |
| Architecture and engineering standards | CTO + Engineering Lead | CTO within approved strategy/budget; CEO for material cost, risk, or strategic change |
| Technical implementation | Engineering Lead / engineer | CTO or delegated reviewer |
| Security risk acceptance or material privacy change | Security Lead + CTO + counsel as needed | CEO |
| Production release | CTO + QA + COO | CEO |
| Incident containment | CTO / Security Lead may take immediate proportionate containment action | Notify CEO immediately; CEO directs material business response |
| Public claims, testimonials, case studies | Marketing + Sales + Delivery | CEO |
| Customer commitments and refunds outside policy | COO + Finance | CEO |
| Accounting/tax filings and statutory positions | Qualified accountant / commercialista | CEO authorises; professional signs/advises as legally required |

## Approval levels
- **Level 0 — Routine execution:** within approved scope, budget, controls, and acceptance criteria. Role owner may proceed and document.
- **Level 1 — Department coordination:** cross-role scheduling or routine operational adjustment within approved plan. COO coordinates; CTO decides technical implementation within remit.
- **Level 2 — Executive approval:** changes to scope, budget, price, deadlines, material architecture, customer commitments, public statements, or risk posture. Escalate to CEO.
- **Level 3 — Stop and seek specialist advice:** legal, tax, regulatory, significant privacy/security incident, suspected fraud, or uncertain professional obligations. Pause affected work; involve qualified professionals and CEO.

## Non-delegable CEO decisions
The CEO retains final approval for company strategy, target market, positioning, business model, service catalogue and material promises, pricing policy and exceptional discounts, major expenditure, contracts and partnerships, hiring and executive appointments, material risk acceptance, significant public claims, production launch/rollback with material business impact, and any decision explicitly reserved.

## Emergency exception
The CTO or Security Lead may take the minimum necessary action to contain an active incident (for example, disable a compromised credential or isolate an affected service). Preserve evidence, avoid unnecessary destruction, and notify CEO and COO immediately. Containment is not permission for broader business, legal, or customer commitments.

## Required decision record
For every Level 2 or Level 3 matter, record: (1) decision requested and deadline; (2) context and evidence; (3) options, including doing nothing; (4) costs, benefits, risks, reversibility; (5) recommendation and dissent; (6) CEO decision and date; (7) owner, implementation plan, and review date.
