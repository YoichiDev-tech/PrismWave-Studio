# Operating Rhythm & Reporting

## Purpose
Keep a small company coordinated without unnecessary meetings. The COO runs the operating rhythm; the CTO owns technical reporting; the CEO sets priorities and makes reserved decisions.

## Daily async update
Each active role posts when useful: completed work; next concrete action; blocker or decision needed; risk or changed estimate. Never imply progress that has not been verified.

## Weekly leadership review
**Participants:** CEO, COO, CTO. Invite role owners when needed.

Agenda: revenue, qualified leads, proposals, active client work and cash; delivery status, quality, defects and feedback; engineering, reliability, security and technical debt; risks, capacity, blockers and deadlines; CEO decisions required; owners and due dates.

COO prepares agenda and action log. CTO prepares technical and risk summary. CEO confirms priorities and decisions.

## Monthly business review
Review revenue received, outstanding invoices, expenses, cash position; lead-to-call and proposal-to-win rates; project margin and delivery variance; customer satisfaction and permissioned testimonials; service performance, incidents, defects and rework; capability versus marketed services; compliance, insurance, contracts and adviser actions.

Finance figures must be reconciled against source records and reviewed by the appropriate accountant where required.

## Project lifecycle
1. **Qualify:** understand problem, constraints, budget, decision process, and fit.
2. **Discover:** document current state, requirements, exclusions, risks, and success criteria.
3. **Propose:** define deliverables, assumptions, timeline, price, payment terms, support, and change process.
4. **Approve:** obtain CEO approval for exceptions and an authorised client agreement before work begins.
5. **Plan:** assign owner, milestones, dependencies, test strategy, and communication cadence.
6. **Build:** use a dedicated branch/environment, peer review, and least-privilege access.
7. **Verify:** test against acceptance criteria and record known limitations.
8. **Release:** use a release checklist, backup/rollback plan, and explicit approval.
9. **Handover:** provide credentials securely, documentation, training, and support terms.
10. **Learn:** request feedback; ask permission before publishing testimonials or case studies.

## Definition of done
Agreed acceptance criteria are met or exceptions explicitly accepted; relevant tests and review are recorded; documentation and handover are complete; security/privacy considerations addressed; client informed of status and limitations; owner and support responsibility clear.

## Escalation
Escalate immediately for suspected credential exposure, data loss, security incident, legal threat, missed critical deadline, material cost overrun, likely client dispute, or any request outside authority.
